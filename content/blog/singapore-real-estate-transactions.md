# Singapore Real Estate Transactions: Complete Self-Help Guide for Buyers and Sellers

This comprehensive guide explains how to buy, sell, or rent property in Singapore without an agent, covering HDB flats, private condominiums, and landed homes. Whether you're a Singapore Citizen, Permanent Resident, or foreigner, this guide will help you understand eligibility requirements, government taxes, and the complete legal process for handling contracts, deposits, and transfers yourself.

## Property Types and Eligibility

Singapore's residential real estate market consists of three main property types, each with different eligibility requirements and regulations.

### HDB Flats (99-Year Lease)

HDB flats are public housing units that form the backbone of Singapore's residential landscape. These properties come with specific eligibility restrictions:

**Who Can Buy:**

- Only Singapore Citizens can purchase new HDB flats (BTO, Sale-of-Balance) independently
- Permanent Residents can only buy resale HDB flats jointly with a Singaporean or under special schemes like the Public or Fiancé schemes
- Foreigners cannot purchase HDB flats

**Important Restrictions:**

- All owners must fulfill a 5-year Minimum Occupation Period (MOP) before selling
- Buyers and sellers must comply with the Ethnic Integration Policy (EIP) and SPR quotas
- HDB restricts the racial composition of owners in each block and neighborhood
- You must verify you qualify under EIP/SPR quotas before proceeding with any transaction

### Private Condominiums and Apartments

Condominiums offer more flexibility in terms of buyer eligibility and represent a significant portion of Singapore's private property market.

**Who Can Buy:**

- Singapore Citizens can purchase freely
- Permanent Residents can purchase without restrictions
- Foreigners can buy condominium units without prior approval
- No Minimum Occupation Period (MOP) requirements
- No Ethnic Integration Policy (EIP) restrictions

**Key Advantage:** Condos provide the most accessible entry point for foreign buyers and investors looking to own property in Singapore.

### Landed Properties

Landed properties include terrace houses, semi-detached homes, and bungalows. These properties have the strictest eligibility requirements.

**Who Can Buy:**

- Only Singapore Citizens may directly purchase landed homes
- Permanent Residents are treated as foreigners and require government approval
- Foreigners must apply to the Singapore Land Authority's Land Dealings Approval Unit
- Approval is usually very limited, often restricted to Sentosa Cove or exceptional cases

**Special Note:** Executive Condominiums (ECs) become similar to private condos after 10 years and may be purchased by foreigners once this milestone is reached.

## Government Taxes and Regulations

Understanding Singapore's property taxes is crucial for budgeting your purchase. The government imposes several stamp duties and financing regulations to manage the property market.

### Buyer's Stamp Duty (BSD)

BSD is a tiered tax applied to all property purchases in Singapore.

**Current BSD Rates for Residential Property:**

- 1% on the first $180,000
- 2% on the next $180,000
- 3% on the next $640,000
- 4% on the next $500,000
- 5% on the next $1,500,000
- 6% on the remaining balance

**Example:** For a $1 million property, you would pay approximately $24,600 in BSD.

### Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty (ABSD)

ABSD is an extra stamp duty designed to cool the property market and prioritize housing for Singaporeans.

**ABSD Rates (as of 2024):**

**Singapore Citizens:**

- First property: 0% ABSD
- Second property: 20% ABSD
- Third and subsequent properties: 30% ABSD

**Permanent Residents:**

- First property: 5% ABSD
- Second property: 30% ABSD
- Third and subsequent properties: 35% ABSD

**Foreigners and Entities:**

- Any residential purchase: 60% ABSD

**Critical Impact:** A foreigner buying a $1 million condominium must pay an additional $600,000 in ABSD on top of the purchase price and BSD.

### Seller's Stamp Duty (SSD)

SSD applies to private property sold within 4 years of purchase, discouraging short-term speculation.

**SSD Rates (for properties acquired on/after 4 July 2025):**

- Sold within 1 year: 16%
- Sold within 2 years: 12%
- Sold within 3 years: 8%
- Sold within 4 years: 4%
- After 4 years: 0%

**Note:** HDB resale transactions are always beyond the 5-year MOP, so SSD does not apply to HDB sales. For properties acquired before 4 July 2025, the previous rates of 12%/8%/4% applied up to 3 years.

### CPF and Financing Rules

**Total Debt Servicing Ratio (TDSR):** Banks enforce a maximum 55% TDSR based on your gross income.

**Loan-to-Value (LTV) Limits:**

- First property: Up to 75% loan
- Second property: Up to 45% loan
- Third property: Up to 35% loan

If you have existing property loans, plan your downpayments carefully. HDB financing and CPF usage have additional rules, including requirements to set aside the Basic Retirement Sum.

### Special ABSD Remission

You can apply for an ABSD refund if you buy a second home and sell your first home within 6 months of the new property's Completion or Certificate of Statutory Completion (CSC) date. This provision helps Singaporean families upgrade without being penalized by double ABSD.

## Buying a Resale HDB Flat (Step-by-Step)

### Step 1: Check Eligibility

Before starting your search, confirm your eligibility:

- Ensure you meet the family nucleus requirements (spouse, citizen parent, etc.)
- Apply for an HDB Flat Eligibility (HFE) letter
- PRs must buy jointly with a Singaporean or PR under approved schemes

### Step 2: Search for Flats

Use multiple resources to find available flats:

- HDB's official Resale Flat directory
- Property portals and websites
- Classified listings

**Critical Step:** Check the block's EIP/SPR quota using HDB's e-service before making an offer. You must not exceed the racial or PR quota by purchasing this flat.

### Step 3: Offer and Option to Purchase (OTP)

Once you've agreed on a price:

- The seller registers an "Intent to Sell" on My HDB Flat Dashboard
- The seller downloads and grants you the official HDB OTP form
- You pay an option fee (up to $1,000)
- You have 21 days to exercise the OTP (expires at 4pm on the expiry date)

### Step 4: Secure Mortgage Eligibility

**For HDB Loan:** Apply for HDB Loan Eligibility (HLE) early in the process.

**For Bank Loan:** Obtain an Approval-In-Principle (AIP) from your chosen bank.

Prepare necessary documents: NRIC, income statements, CPF statements, tax notices, etc.

### Step 5: Submit Resale Application

After exercising the OTP:

- Both buyer and seller submit a joint Resale Application online via My HDB Flat Dashboard
- Upload all required forms (Declaration of Medical Condition, HLE letter, etc.)
- Pay resale fees
- HDB processes the application (approximately 8 weeks)

### Step 6: Completion

Once HDB approves the resale:

- Attend the HDB Resale Completion appointment with all parties (seller, buyer, bank representative, lawyer if applicable, HDB officer)
- Pay the remaining purchase sum via cashier's order
- HDB transfers the flat to your name
- Collect the keys

## Buying a New HDB Flat (BTO)

Build-To-Order (BTO) flats are sold via ballot to eligible first-time buyers with at least one Singaporean in the household. The BTO process includes:

- Applying during HDB sales launches
- Meeting income ceilings
- Waiting 3-4 years for construction completion

This process is handled entirely through HDB's portal. For immediate occupancy, consider resale HDB flats instead.

## Buying Resale Private Property (Condominium/Landed)

### Step 1: Verify Eligibility

- Citizens and PRs can buy most condos and landed properties
- Foreigners can buy condos but need special approval for landed properties
- Ensure your CPF savings and financing are in order

### Step 2: Find Your Property

Search through:

- Online property marketplaces
- Direct seller listings
- Property portals

Arrange viewings and verify the land title and tenure (freehold vs. leasehold) through SLA's Integrated Land Information Service if needed.

### Step 3: Negotiate and Secure OTP

After deciding to purchase:

- Negotiate price and terms with the seller
- Seller grants an Option to Purchase (OTP) upon payment of 1-2% option fee
- The OTP gives you 2-3 weeks to decide (typically 14-21 days for private properties)

### Step 4: Engage a Lawyer and Conduct Title Search

**Immediately after signing the OTP:**

- Hire a licensed conveyancing lawyer
- Lawyer conducts a title search for encumbrances (caveats, mortgages, etc.)
- Lawyer confirms the seller has good title
- Lawyer lodges a caveat on the property to protect your interest before you pay the 20% deposit

### Step 5: Exercise the OTP

To proceed with the purchase:

- Pay the remaining deposit (totaling 20% including the option fee) at your lawyer's office
- Sign the Sale & Purchase Agreement (S&P) provided by the seller's lawyer
- Your lawyer prepares to stamp the documents with IRAS

### Step 6: Pay Stamp Duties

Pay all stamp duties (BSD and ABSD) to IRAS within 14 days of signing the OTP/S&P. Your lawyer can handle e-Stamping on your behalf. If you plan to sell your existing home within 6 months, you may apply for an ABSD refund later.

### Step 7: Completion

On the completion date specified in the S&P:

- Meet at the seller's lawyer's office
- Pay the remaining 80% balance via cashier's order
- Your lawyer ensures proper transfer of title
- Seller provides vacant possession
- You receive a new Certificate of Title in your name

## Buying New-Launch Private Property

Purchasing directly from developers involves a different process:

### Step 1: Register Interest

Before the official launch:

- Submit a Letter of Authorization (LOA) with a blank cheque for the booking fee
- Enter the buyer lottery system

### Step 2: Book Your Unit

If selected in the ballot:

- Pay a 5% booking fee
- Review the Property Details Information (PDI) booklet
- Developer issues the OTP
- **Note:** If you back out at the OTP stage, typically 25% of your 5% deposit is forfeited

### Step 3: Sign the Sale & Purchase Agreement

Within approximately 2 weeks of receiving the OTP:

- Developer's lawyers prepare the formal S&P
- Sign the S&P at your lawyer's office
- Pay an additional 15% (totaling 20% including the initial 5%) within 5 weeks
- Your lawyer returns the signed S&P to the developer
- Payments are held in escrow for future milestone payments

### Step 4: Progress Payments

As construction reaches specific milestones:

- Pay remaining installments (usually 75% of the purchase price)
- Follow the payment schedule outlined in the S&P
- Payments typically include stages like foundation, superstructure, and Temporary Occupation Permit (TOP)

### Step 5: Handover

Upon Completion/CSC:

- Pay any final outstanding sums
- Your lawyer releases funds from escrow
- Receive title and keys from the developer

**Important:** Engaging a lawyer early is strongly advised for all private property purchases. Your lawyer ensures proper conveyancing, including title searches, caveat lodging, and documentation.

## Sub-Sale Transactions (Pre-Completion Resale)

Sub-sales occur when the original buyer of a new-launch unit resells it before the project is completed.

**How Sub-Sales Work:**

1. The original buyer must have already signed the S&P with the developer and paid stamp duties (BSD/ABSD)
2. The original buyer notifies the developer of their intent to resell
3. The seller (original buyer) and new buyer sign a fresh OTP between themselves
4. The developer enters into a new S&P with the new buyer on the same terms as the original agreement
5. The original buyer pays Seller's Stamp Duty as if it were a normal resale (e.g., 12% if selling within 1 year)
6. Upon project completion (TOP/CSC), the new buyer receives the keys
7. The original buyer's obligations end when the new S&P is signed

Sub-sales are more complex due to developer involvement but follow the same general conveyancing rules once the new S&P is executed.

## Selling Your Property

### Selling an HDB Flat

**Prerequisites:**

- You must have fulfilled the 5-year Minimum Occupation Period (MOP)
- Verify you meet all eligibility requirements to sell

**Process:**

1. Register your intent to sell on My HDB Flat Dashboard
2. The system ensures you meet quotas and notifies you when you can grant an OTP
3. Negotiate price and terms with your buyer
4. Grant the HDB OTP using HDB's prescribed form
5. Accept the OTP fee (up to $1,000) and set an exercise date
6. After the buyer exercises the OTP, both parties submit the joint Resale Application to HDB
7. Endorse all required documents and pay fees
8. Attend the HDB Resale Completion appointment
9. HDB transfers ownership to the buyer

### Selling Private Property (Condominium/Landed)

**Process:**

1. Advertise or list your property for sale through various channels
2. When a buyer is found, grant an OTP and collect an option fee (typically 1-2%)
3. Once the buyer exercises the OTP (pays 20% deposit), engage conveyancing lawyers
4. Your lawyer liaises with the buyer's lawyer to finalize the Sale & Purchase Agreement
5. Ensure all property taxes, maintenance fees, and estate expenses are settled
6. On completion day, receive the sale proceeds from the buyer's lawyer
7. Hand over vacant possession

**Critical Reminder:** Only sign official OTP or S&P forms. Verify all amounts (balance payments, stamp duties) with your lawyer beforehand. The sale is only legally valid once the document is stamped by IRAS.

## Renting Properties

Singapore has strict regulations governing rental properties to maintain housing quality and community standards.

### Renting Out HDB Flats

**Eligibility:**

- Only Singapore Citizens may rent out their entire HDB flat after fulfilling the 5-year MOP
- All flat types (3-room and above) can rent out individual bedrooms, including PR/foreign owners

**Requirements:**

- All rentals require HDB approval via e-service
- Minimum lease term: 6 months
- Maximum lease term: 3 years (2 years if tenant is foreign/PR)
- Nationality quotas apply: maximum 11% non-Malaysian foreigners/PRs per block (8% per neighborhood)
- Always register the tenancy with HDB
- Charge a security deposit (commonly 1-2 months' rent)
- Use a written lease agreement

### Renting Out Private Property (Condominium/Landed)

**Requirements:**

- Minimum rental period: 3 months (short-term leases not allowed)
- Occupancy cap: Properties under 90 sqm are limited to 6 unrelated occupants
- Temporary relief: Properties 90 sqm and above can accommodate 8 occupants until December 2026

**Landlord Responsibilities:**

- Prepare a comprehensive tenancy agreement specifying rent, duration, deposit, maintenance responsibilities, etc.
- Ensure tenants have legal right to stay in Singapore (valid work passes, etc.)
- Collect security deposit (typically 1-2 months' rent)
- Report rental income for tax purposes
- Pay applicable property tax

**General Rule:** Both HDB and private properties prohibit "short-term" stays (less than 6 months for HDB, less than 3 months for private property) unless you hold a hotel license.

Always conduct property inspections before and after each tenancy, and strictly adhere to the lease terms.

## Legal and Financial Checklist

### 1. Engage a Lawyer

For any property sale or purchase, hire a licensed conveyancing lawyer from the outset. Your lawyer will:

- Conduct title searches
- Verify the seller's ownership is clear and unencumbered
- Lodge a caveat on the title to protect your interest
- Handle the Sale & Purchase Agreement
- Manage the title transfer process
- Ensure all documentation is properly stamped

**Never attempt to draft your own sale contract.**

### 2. CPF Usage

If using CPF funds for your purchase:

- Ensure you meet HDB/CPF withdrawal requirements
- Set aside the Basic Retirement Sum if applicable
- Understand that buying multiple properties may impose additional Total Debt Servicing Ratio restrictions on CPF usage (separate from bank TDSR)

### 3. Financing Approval

Secure mortgage approval or proof of funds before making any commitments:

- Banks will assess your entire debt profile
- Remember the 55% TDSR limit
- Account for LTV limits based on the number of properties you own

### 4. Stamp Duty Payment

- Buyers must pay BSD and ABSD promptly (within 14 days)
- Sellers pay SSD if applicable when selling
- IRAS provides e-Stamping services through lawyers
- Always keep receipts and documentation

### 5. Title Transfer

On completion day:

- Your lawyer registers the title transfer with the Singapore Land Authority
- You receive a new Title Certificate in your name
- Collect keys and verify vacant possession is as agreed in the contract

## Summary of Key Points

### Eligibility

- Singapore Citizens and PRs can buy most private properties
- Only Singapore Citizens can buy new HDB flats or landed properties without restrictions
- Foreigners can purchase condominiums but not HDB flats
- Foreigners need special approval for landed properties (rarely granted outside Sentosa Cove)

### Taxes and Fees

- Budget for Buyer's Stamp Duty (up to 6% of purchase price)
- Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty ranges from 0% to 60% depending on citizenship and number of properties
- Sellers must pay Seller's Stamp Duty (up to 16%) if selling within 4 years of purchase
- Always verify the latest rates on IRAS.gov.sg

### Process

- Use official forms: HDB's MyFlat Dashboard for HDB transactions, OTP and S&P with lawyers for private deals
- Never skip the lawyer's role in conveyancing, stamping, and title transfer
- Follow all deadlines strictly (OTP expiry, stamp duty filing within 14 days of signing)
- Engage professionals early in the process

### Rentals

- Obtain all necessary approvals before renting
- Follow tenancy laws carefully
- Short-term lets (less than 3-6 months) are illegal for ordinary landlords
- Check eligibility requirements and quotas for HDB properties
- Verify occupancy caps for private properties

## Final Advice

By following these comprehensive guidelines and consulting the official sources cited throughout this guide, you can confidently transact real estate in Singapore on your own. The key to success is thorough preparation, understanding your obligations, and engaging qualified professionals (especially conveyancing lawyers) at the appropriate stages.

Always verify the latest regulations on:

- **HDB.gov.sg** for public housing rules
- **URA.gov.sg** for urban planning and private property regulations
- **IRAS.gov.sg** for current stamp duty rates and tax obligations
- **SLA.gov.sg** for land authority matters and foreign ownership rules

Property regulations in Singapore are subject to updates, so staying informed about current policies is essential for every transaction.
